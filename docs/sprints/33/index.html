Meaning as Vectors: Word Embeddings

A word becomes a direction in space. Close means the same company, not the same meaning.

In short. From one-hot to dense vectors, the idea that a word is known by the company it keeps, predicting against counting, and how to read word analogies.

  2. 2Offset 12 country → capital offsets lie 21–45° from their mean; 12 random pairs lie 77–109°. Sides are for drawing only.
  3. 3Skip The nearest word to king − man + woman is king. Queen wins only when the three inputs are skipped.

01 One-Hot to Dense Represent

  • One-hot: one 1 in 400 000 slots; every pair at 90°, so dog is no closer to cat than democracy
  • Dense: 50 learned numbers; cat–dog 22.8°, cat–democracy 87.9°
  • Learned inside a language model against “the curse of dimensionality” (Bengio et al. 2003)

Normalise first: cosine reads direction, not length.

02 The Company It Keeps Use

  • Harris (1954): “difference of meaning correlates with difference of distribution”
  • Firth (1957): “You shall know a word by the company it keeps!”
  • Count neighbours, reweight by PMI, compress by SVD (Deerwester et al. 1990)

Meaning here is a pattern of use; the vectors never see the world.

03 Predict or Count W2V · GloVe

  • Skip-gram: vectors “good at predicting the nearby words” (Mikolov et al. 2013)
  • GloVe: dot products fitted to log co-occurrence counts (Pennington et al. 2014)
  • With negative sampling it implicitly factorises shifted PMI (Levy & Goldberg 2014)

Tuning explains much of the gap (Levy et al. 2015).

04 Reading Analogies 3CosAdd

  • king − man + woman: the nearest word is king (0.885); queen (0.852) is second
  • Queen wins only if the inputs are skipped: “we discard the input words” (Mikolov et al.)
  • man : doctor :: woman : ? gives doctor (0.905); nurse needs the skip (Nissim et al. 2020)

Inputs allowed: 3CosAdd falls 0.71 → 0.21 (Nissim et al.); Linzen (2016) got an input back 98% of the time.

05 Try It Yourself PYTHON 3.13.16

sprint33_meaning_as_vectors_word_embeddings.py

import numpy as np, gensim.downloader as api
wv = api.load("glove-wiki-gigaword-50")  # 66 MB, once
V, w, ix = wv.vectors, wv.index_to_key, wv.key_to_index
E = V / np.linalg.norm(V, axis=1)[:, None]  # unit rows
u = lambda t: E[ix[t]]
print(len(w), "words x", E.shape[1], "numbers")
for t in ["dog", "kitten", "car", "democracy"]:  # 1
    c = u("cat") @ u(t)  # cosine as an angle
    print(f"cat-{t}: cos {c:.3f} = "
          f"{np.degrees(np.arccos(c)):.1f} deg")
P = """france paris italy rome spain madrid germany
berlin japan tokyo russia moscow greece athens egypt
cairo morocco rabat turkey ankara china beijing
poland warsaw""".split()  # 2 country -> capital
def mcos(D):  # mean cosine between the offsets
    D = D / np.linalg.norm(D, axis=1)[:, None]
    return (D @ D.T)[np.triu_indices(len(D), 1)].mean()
C = np.array([u(t) for t in P])
D = C[1::2] - C[::2]  # 12 country -> capital offsets
r = np.random.default_rng(33).integers(1000, 20000,
                                       (12, 2))
R = E[r[:, 1]] - E[r[:, 0]]  # 12 random word pairs
print(f"offsets: capitals {mcos(D):.2f}, "
      f"random pairs {mcos(R):.2f}")
def top(q, x=(), k=2):  # 3 analogy by 3CosAdd
    s = E @ q / np.linalg.norm(q)
    o = [i for i in np.argsort(-s)[:9] if w[i] not in x]
    return " ".join(f"{w[i]} {s[i]:.3f}" for i in o[:k])
for a, b, c in [("man", "king", "woman"),
                ("man", "doctor", "woman")]:
    q = u(b) - u(a) + u(c)  # x = skip the 3 inputs
    print(f"{b}: {top(q)} | skip: {top(q, {a,b,c}, 1)}")
o = np.argsort(-(E @ u("hot")))  # 4 one point per word
print("hot ->", " ".join(w[i] for i in o[1:7]))
n = list(np.argsort(-(E @ u("bank")))).index(ix["river"])
print("bank -> river is neighbour no.", n)

06 General Notes Practice

  • Close is not same: cold is hot’s 2nd neighbour; SimLex-999 rates similarity, not association (Hill et al. 2015)
  • One point per word: river is bank’s 2 353rd neighbour
  • Vectors learn their corpus, stereotypes included (Caliskan et al. 2017)
