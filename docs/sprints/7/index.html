Four Minds on Science

How does science change when the facts push back? Four answers, 1934–1975. Popper · Kuhn · Lakatos · Feyerabend

In short. Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos and Feyerabend, with one idea to keep from each; Uranus and Neptune against Mercury and the planet Vulcan that was never found.

Sprint

Fig. 1 in words

  1. Same rescue move, two outcomes. Lakatos asks: did the fix predict anything new?

    • top: Newton’s gravity · meets an orbit that won’t fit
    • left branch: Uranus drifts · add an unseen planet → Neptune found · 1846 · new fact
    • under the left branch: progressive
    • right branch: Mercury +43″/century · add planet “Vulcan” → Never found · the patch fails → General relativity · 1915 · explains 43″

1 Popper 1934

The Logic of Scientific Discovery

  • Scientific means refutable: some result could sink it
  • No pile of confirmations proves a law

Keep: ask what would prove me wrong.

2 Kuhn 1962

The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

  • Normal science solves puzzles inside a paradigm
  • Anomalies pile up, crisis, revolution
  • Rival paradigms resist direct comparison

Keep: name the paradigm I work in.

3 Lakatos 1970

Research programmes

  • A hard core, shielded by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses
  • Progressive: fixes predict new facts. Degenerating: fixes only rescue

Keep: judge a fix by what it predicts.

4 Feyerabend 1975

Against Method

  • No single method runs through the history of science
  • “Anything goes” was his picture of a rationalist’s alarm, not a rule he held

Keep: give rival ideas a real hearing.

5 Try It Yourself

sprint07_four_minds_on_science.py

from math import pi
GM, c = 1.327e20, 2.998e8
a, e = 5.791e10, 0.2056
d = 6*pi*GM/(c*c*a*(1-e*e))
orbits = 36525 / 87.969
print(d * orbits * 206265)

Output, as printed on the image

42.97  (arcsec / century)
