Byte Pair Encoding & Its Edge Cases

A tokenizer is a list of merges. Change the list or its order, and words split differently.

In short. The merge loop, how ties decide the result, rare, capitalised and unseen words, and why numbers split oddly.

  2. 2Ties 6 of 10 steps were ties. My code keeps the first pair it met.

01 The Merge Loop Algorithm

  • Gage (1994) compressed files by swapping the most frequent byte pair for an unused byte
  • Sennrich et al. (2016) merge letters instead, plus an end-of-word mark
  • Vocabulary = base symbols + merges, the only setting

Pairs never cross word boundaries. New words replay the merges in the order they were learned.

02 Ties Decide Order

  • Steps 1, 2, 4, 6, 7 and 10 had tied pairs
  • Break ties alphabetically: the corpus still ends at 28, but newer becomes ne|w|e|r|_
  • Different merge list, different pieces, different IDs

The tie rule is part of the tokenizer. Write it down.

03 Rare, Cased, Unseen Edges

  • newer stays in 4 pieces: “er” never won a merge
  • Lowest: L was never seen, so low cannot form
  • Under-trained tokens sit in the vocabulary but were barely seen in training

Land & Bartolo (2024): SolidGoldMagikarp-like tokens, found across many models.

04 Numbers Split Oddly Digits

  • GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 cut numbers into chunks of up to 3 digits, from the left
  • Commas force groups from the right: GPT-3.5 addition went from 68.5% to 95.6%
  • LLaMA and PaLM split numbers into single digits

Singh & Strouse (2024), a preprint: the cut alone moved accuracy, one-shot.

05 Try It Yourself PYTHON 3.11.15

sprint23_byte_pair_encoding_edge_cases.py

import re
from collections import Counter
vocab = {"l o w _": 5, "l o w e r _": 2,
  "n e w e s t _": 6, "w i d e s t _": 3}
def merge(pair, w):            # a b -> ab
    a = re.escape(" ".join(pair))
    return re.sub(rf"(?<!\S){a}(?!\S)",
                  "".join(pair), w)
merges = []
for step in range(10):
    pairs = Counter()
    for w, n in vocab.items():
        s = w.split()
        for p in zip(s, s[1:]):
            pairs[p] += n
    best = max(pairs, key=pairs.get)
    merges.append(best)
    vocab = {merge(best, w): n
             for w, n in vocab.items()}
def bpe(word):
    w = " ".join(word) + " _"
    for pair in merges:
        w = merge(pair, w)
    return w.replace(" ", "|")
print(" ".join("".join(p) for p in merges))
print(sum(len(w.split()) * n
          for w, n in vocab.items()))
for w in ["lowest", "newer", "Lowest"]:
    print(w, bpe(w))

06 General Notes Practice

  • Print the merge list and read it
  • Test capitals, leading spaces, digits, emoji and Arabic
  • BPE gives one fixed split. Dropping merges at random in training gave up to 2.3 BLEU (Provilkov et al. 2020)

Unigram LM tokenization matched or beat BPE in pretraining (Bostrom & Durrett 2020).
